Nereus

Ships at sea. What we are building now.

The story

In the Greek story, Nereus, the old man of the sea, would answer any question truthfully if you could hold on to him while he changed shape: a lion, a serpent, water. Heracles held on.

Ship data changes shape the same way, and the job is to hold on until it tells you what happened.

What it does

We read AIS from several networks, keep every report as it was received, and test each one against a calibrated model of what that ship could have done since its last report.

Where many ships go wrong in one place, we open a single event and describe it: where they were, where they said they were, when, and how far apart. Every finding goes into a record with the reports behind it and a script that recomputes the numbers.

Who it is for

People who need to know where a ship really was and may have to prove it to someone else: charterers and owners in a dispute, claims handlers and P&I correspondents, admiralty lawyers and the experts they hire.

We are working with a few design partners on real cases now.

Where it stands

Working today: live scoring of AIS against ship physics, clustering of area events like Lima, and records whose numbers anyone can rerun.

Still to come: our own receivers at chokepoints, trusted timestamps on the archive, and purchased radar imagery for disputed cases.
